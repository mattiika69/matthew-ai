# Worked reasoning, with evidence boundaries

## A real public content-agency audit

**Source type:** Recorded public audit; no access to the business's private performance data. The business is left unnamed in this reusable reference because its identity adds little to the lesson; the original recording remains linked.

Matthew first identifies the industry, intended buyer and offer. He notices different buyer revenue levels across assets and conflicting view promises/timeframes. He acknowledges unusually strong production and proof rather than dismissing the entire funnel.

Source: S003 · Locator: 00:31–05:44; 09:22. [Audit setup](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h1D4F8AXWS4&t=31s).

**What he sees:** An unclear, widely ranged promise and inconsistent buyer/result language; the buyer has to work too hard to understand what is sold. Benefits may exist operationally, but hidden information does not help the prospect decide. The analysis is limited to what a prospect can see.

Source: S003 · Locator: 05:15–05:44; 09:49–10:21; 18:18–18:48. [Diagnosis](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h1D4F8AXWS4&t=315s).

**What he prioritizes:** More consistent messaging, rebuilt above-the-fold presentation, enough written offer detail, congruency across the journey, and a VSL that reaches the point faster. The page should be understandable without watching the whole video.

Source: S003 · Locator: 38:50–43:26; 1:12:06–1:13:14. [Priorities](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h1D4F8AXWS4&t=4326s).

**What this proves:** Matthew's process for reviewing public-facing clarity and proposing changes. **What it does not prove:** a measured conversion lift, the business's profitability, or that the proposed promises are feasible for a different business. No post-rebuild test result is provided.

**Correction:** The original description and spoken self-correction specify **two hours per month**, not two hours per week. Preserve the correction when discussing the demo; do not copy either time commitment into a member's offer without evidence.

Source: S003 · Locator: 31:33; 33:50; original video description. [Corrected effort statement](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=h1D4F8AXWS4&t=1893s).

**Transferable judgment (synthesis):** An asset-only review can make a strong clarity recommendation without claiming a proven business bottleneck. Keep what is working, isolate the inconsistency, propose a test and define the downstream measure separately.

## A hypothetical annuity-lead ad demonstration

Matthew explicitly invents an agency selling leads to financial advisors to demonstrate turning an offer into an ad and page. The demonstration addresses concerns about integration, speed and replacements, then aligns the page/VSL with the ad.

Source: S004 · Locator: 07:17–12:32; 21:14–25:12. [Worked ad](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qhAHUM1-l1c&t=437s).

**Useful reasoning:** Start with a clear buyer/result, choose objections from customer understanding, make the next step understandable, and retain the same promise across assets.

**Boundary:** This is not a verified winning ad. The example's order minimum, delivery time and replacement terms are not factual claims about the member's business. Transfer the reasoning, not the fabricated demonstration terms.

## An economics exception inside the offer course

Matthew first introduces an 80% gross-margin check when a business is below his 2× upfront-cash objective. Later, his worked model has 70% margin and meets the 2× objective, which he accepts.

Source: S001 · Locator: 2:05:06–2:05:50; 2:13:01–2:13:09. [Exception](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HS8EjMBhG8o&t=7981s).

**Useful reasoning:** The intermediate target serves the economic objective. Do not push a member to maximize a component rate when the actual objective is already met and the change may damage another part of the business.

**Boundary:** The model is illustrative, not a client case with observed results. Validate the member's cash, cost basis and fulfillment capacity before applying the conclusion.
