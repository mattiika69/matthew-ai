# Economics and contextual benchmarks

These are dated teaching heuristics and reported experience, not industry-wide empirical norms. Establish business type, buyer, traffic temperature, maturity, period, sample size and denominator before comparing a member's numbers. Use the [calculator definitions](metric-definitions.md) for arithmetic, not as evidence of Matthew's opinions.

## Cash collection is different from booked revenue

For the **front-end intro offer sold to cold traffic**, Matthew's growth-design target is **upfront cash collected at least twice combined customer acquisition and fulfillment cost**. His course explicitly excludes upsells and recurring services from its offer-design scope; do not present this target as his universal rule for every revenue stream. It leaves cash to fund delivery and the next acquisition. Contract value, revenue recognized and money received on different dates are not interchangeable.

Source: S001 · Locator: 09:05–09:31. [Front-end offer scope](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HS8EjMBhG8o&t=545s).
Source: S001 · Locator: 1:53:09–2:03:37. [Cash timing](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HS8EjMBhG8o&t=6789s).

The progression matters: first make a sale; then reach roughly 1× combined acquisition and fulfillment cost; then improve delivery efficiency, acquisition, proof and price toward 2×. Matthew explicitly acknowledges delivery capacity, and that CAC can rise when ads reach broader audiences. The target does not guarantee unlimited scale or eliminate fixed costs and execution risk.

Source: S001 · Locator: 2:01:00–2:03:55. [Progression and capacity](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HS8EjMBhG8o&t=7260s).

**Application (synthesis):** For “should I scale?”, examine collected cash, acquisition cost basis, delivery cost/timing, capacity and whether observed results are likely to persist at higher spend. If one rate looks poor but customer economics work, do not automatically rebuild that stage just to meet a benchmark. Conversely, high ROAS is insufficient when fulfillment or collection timing consumes the cash.

## Preserve conflicting contexts

The offer course is dated September 14, 2026; the funnel course September 25, 2026. For a live-call funnel diagnosis, the later, explicit attended-call discussion is the more directly applicable source. For explaining the earlier cash-target checklist, retain its 20–25% target. Neither supports saying all businesses below a chosen percentage are broken.

The 80% margin check is also conditional. In the same offer lesson Matthew accepts a 70% margin model that meets the larger cash objective. This illustrates prioritizing the governing economic outcome over perfect intermediate metrics.

Source: S001 · Locator: 2:05:06–2:05:50; 2:13:01–2:13:09. [Outcome over checklist](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HS8EjMBhG8o&t=7506s).

## What not to infer

- A 2× cash target is not the same as 2× ROAS: its denominator includes acquisition **and fulfillment**.
- AOV does not establish LTV; gross revenue does not establish contribution or profit.
- A high close rate may reflect low price, warm leads, strong qualification or a tiny sample; it does not alone authorize raising price.
- A high share of experienced buyers among retained customers does not reveal the retention probability of inexperienced buyers. The source's 5%/9% inverse-probability inference is unsupported; see [source cautions](sources.md).
- Mathematical breakeven is a ceiling under stated assumptions, not necessarily a prudent target after overhead and uncertainty.

These distinctions are standard arithmetic/measurement reasoning and curator interpretation, not additional Matthew quotations.
