# Funnel execution: bookings, shows and sales

Use this reference after identifying the weak stage. These are Matthew's call-based funnel teachings; apply to the member's actual funnel and distinguish a diagnosed failure from a plausible test.

## Applications and lead quality

Qualification should screen for ability and intent to buy and should connect to downstream conversion quality. More applications are not automatically better. Matthew allows an early learning exception when first getting started with paid ads: the first roughly 10–20 paid-ad calls can use less qualification to learn how those calls go, before tightening it. Do not use this as a permanent instruction to send unsuitable leads to sales.

Source: S002 · Locator: 58:47–1:00:19. [Application purpose](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ra3_QNPzBeA&t=3527s).

He examines whether prior lead-buying experience correlates with repeat purchases in his lead business. Use the qualitative question—what buyer traits predict successful use and retention?—without repeating the source's invalid inverse-probability calculation. Details and correction are in [sources](sources.md).

Source: S002 · Locator: 1:04:08–1:04:28. [Experience and activation](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ra3_QNPzBeA&t=3848s).

**Application (inference):** Compare qualified and unqualified lead cohorts through sales and retention before removing a qualification question to improve CPL. Check data volume and attribution. A lower form-completion rate can be worthwhile when cost per suitable customer improves.

## Qualified people apply but do not book

Distinguish all applicants from qualified applicants, then inspect the calendar handoff and available slots. Matthew routes disqualified applicants to content/newsletter rather than automatically booking them. For qualified applicants who abandon scheduling, use follow-up to recover the booking.

Source: S002 · Locator: 1:14:27–1:24:35. [Booking and recovery](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ra3_QNPzBeA&t=4467s).

Availability matters: he generally favors plenty of slots and calls within about 72 hours. He also describes massive-scale situations with 7–14-day waits, and a separate advanced tradeoff where at least 24 hours allows pre-call education. Preserve these as context-dependent choices, not contradictory instructions to apply simultaneously.

Source: S002 · Locator: 1:15:25–1:18:04; 1:25:29. [Timing tradeoffs](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ra3_QNPzBeA&t=4525s).

**Next useful evidence:** qualified applications, scheduled calls, available times, time to first personal contact, and whether recovery is actually performed. If calendar access is not supplied, do not claim to have inspected it.

## Calls booked but people do not show

Matthew distinguishes speed-to-lead and show rate. In this portion of the course, speed-to-lead means booking to first human outreach, so establish the local definition rather than mixing it with form-submission response time.

Source: S002 · Locator: 1:26:18–1:26:59. [Show-stage metrics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ra3_QNPzBeA&t=5178s).

The pre-call phase has two jobs: get the person to attend and prepare them to understand the offer. Reminders handle appointment logistics; educational messages answer major questions and objections. Improve delivery before endlessly changing email copy, then subject/preview and relevant content. Sequence FAQs so prerequisite understanding comes first.

Source: S002 · Locator: 1:29:29–1:39:17. [Pre-call education](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ra3_QNPzBeA&t=5369s).

Matthew describes contact from the actual closer and a short casual video as ways to create familiarity, with clear responsibility and notifications. Do not represent a generic video as uniquely recorded for the individual. He also considers offering a sooner slot when appropriate.

Source: S002 · Locator: 1:40:28–1:46:52. [Personal contact and ownership](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ra3_QNPzBeA&t=6028s).

A no-show sequence should get the prospect to rebook and then attend. Do not merely repeat a sales pitch or classify future appointments as no-shows.

Source: S002 · Locator: 1:58:42. [No-show purpose](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ra3_QNPzBeA&t=7122s). The future-appointment distinction is standard measurement hygiene.

## People show but do not buy

Use attended calls when evaluating live-call close rate. A booked-call denominator combines attendance and closing and can serve forecasting, but it is not the same metric. As scale and offer mix grow, revenue per lead or call can be more useful than chasing the highest closing percentage.

Source: S002 · Locator: 2:00:31–2:01:43. [Sales metrics](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ra3_QNPzBeA&t=7231s).

A founder's intuitive selling may not transfer to hired closers. Matthew calls for a repeatable process that can be reviewed. **Application (inference):** inspect actual calls for qualification, offer understanding, unresolved objections and the next step before blaming the closer or lowering price.

Source: S002 · Locator: 2:08:42. [Transferable selling](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ra3_QNPzBeA&t=7722s).

When a sale does not close on the call, establish the next meeting while still together when appropriate. Post-call follow-up can address concerns using relevant case studies; distinguish an absolute growth example from a percentage improvement and keep the original context. Manual follow-up is useful when performed consistently; automation is better than a supposedly ideal process nobody executes.

Source: S002 · Locator: 2:14:57; 2:20:37–2:24:35. [Next step and follow-up](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ra3_QNPzBeA&t=8097s).

Payment documents and onboarding expectations must match the call. Clarify meaningful delivery milestones and send the agreement/payment step promptly; inconsistent promises destroy confidence. Never invent 24-hour, 48-hour or seven-day commitments just because those durations appear in a demonstration.

Source: S002 · Locator: 2:27:05–2:29:35. [Handoff](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Ra3_QNPzBeA&t=8825s).
